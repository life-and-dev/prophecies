# Zechariah 12:10: The Pierced One

Zechariah 12:10 describes mourning for one who was pierced. John applies its piercing language to Jesus.

## The Prophecy

> “I will pour out ... a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, so that, when they look on me, on him whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him.” — Zechariah 12:10 (ESV)

Zechariah 12:1-9 frames the verse as an oracle concerning Jerusalem. The LORD makes Jerusalem a burden to surrounding peoples, strikes hostile forces, strengthens Judah, and promises to destroy nations that come against Jerusalem. The mourning continues in 12:11 with a comparison to Hadad-rimmon in the plain of Megiddo, an allusion commonly connected with lament for Josiah but interpreted differently.

The verse moves from “me” to “him whom they have pierced” and then mourning “for him”. The Masoretic Text has this wording, while ancient versions, proposed textual readings, and translations handle the syntax differently, including readings such as looking to the LORD concerning the pierced one. The variants and shift require care: the text names YHWH as speaker but does not by itself explain every relation between YHWH and the pierced figure.

## Christian View

John 19:34-37 applies the pierced-one wording to Jesus' crucifixion. John 19:34-35 also stresses the eyewitness report: a soldier pierces Jesus' side, blood and water come out, and the witness testifies that the account is true. Revelation 1:7 connects the wording with a future public mourning. Christians therefore may distinguish an initial fulfilment in the crucifixion from a future, national, or wider fulfilment, rather than treating the two New Testament uses as competing.

Trinitarians infer from the first-person language that the pierced Jesus shares YHWH's identity. This inference has force for readers who treat John's application as carrying the speaker identity forward. It goes beyond the bare fact that John identifies Jesus as the pierced one, and it must still account for Zechariah's “me/him” distinction.

## Non-Christian / Secular / Jewish Views

Jewish and critical readings place the mourning amid Jerusalem's eschatological warfare. Proposed referents for the pierced one include fallen soldiers, a leader, a representative figure, Israel collectively, or the war-dead. The Hadad-rimmon comparison reinforces a setting of public lament, though its precise historical reference is debated. Some Jewish readings expect a future national mourning rather than identify the verse with a past crucifixion.

The “me/him” shift and textual variants prevent a simple identification of the pierced figure with YHWH. A Unitarian reading can accept John's application to Jesus while distinguishing YHWH, who pours out grace and supplication, from the mourned-for representative. Revelation's future use may then be read as a further application or as part of a possible dual fulfilment. The Trinitarian identity conclusion remains an inference rather than the text's explicit statement.

Potter imagery sometimes grouped with this prophecy belongs to Zechariah 11, not Zechariah 12; see [Zechariah 11:12-13](zechariah-11-12.draft.md#the-prophecy).

## Conclusion

John's application supports Jesus as the pierced one. The Trinitarian identity inference and the [representative reading](#non-christian-secular-jewish-views) differ over whether Zechariah directly identifies him with YHWH.
