# Revelation 5:13: Honour for God and the Lamb

Revelation 5 gives universal blessing and honour to the one on the throne and the Lamb. This draft considers the honour and distinction in the vision.

## The Prophecy

> “To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honour and glory and might forever and ever!” — Revelation 5:13 (ESV)

The throne-sitter holds the sealed scroll. The Lamb receives it because he was slain and redeemed people for God (Revelation 5:6-10); then every creature honours both recipients.

## Christian View

Trinitarians argue that universal honour and the elders’ response establish that the Lamb receives worship due to God. In Revelation 5:8, the elders “fell down” (*piptō*) before the Lamb. In Revelation 5:14, they again fall down and *proskyneō*, a term which can [denote worship or homage](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/worship) according to context. Revelation 4:10 and 7:11 depict heavenly worship of God, while Revelation 19:10 and 22:8-9 forbid *proskyneō* directed to angels. Revelation 5 therefore appears to include the Lamb in the heavenly worship offered to God.

Revelation 22:13's speaker is also debated in light of 22:16, where Jesus speaks. This may support application of divine titles to Jesus. Revelation 19 portrays the victorious rider, and Revelation 5 portrays the Lamb, as sharing God's final victory.

## Non-Christian / Secular / Jewish Views

A Unitarian reading gives full weight to the Lamb's heavenly honour but distinguishes him from the one seated on the throne. The Lamb receives the scroll, not because he is the throne-sitter, but because he was slain.

Revelation 4, 7, 19, and 22 distinguish God, Lamb, angels, and servants. Revelation 22:16 identifies Jesus as a closing speaker but does not by itself resolve 22:13 or identify the Lamb as God Almighty in chapter 5. The vision can show delegated exaltation and shared honour without identity.
